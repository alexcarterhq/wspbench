**WSPBench is a small-network research preview.** Its protocol separates A: no external tools, B: bounded computational weight analysis, and C: instrumented diagnosis/repair. The released model pilot measures A only. CPU checks and conventional baselines for B/C do not establish model performance under those conditions.

The challenge targets used by the pilot are constructed four-stage Boolean ReLU microcircuits with 4,049 persistent float32 scalars and ten binary inputs. They are not end-to-end trained transformers. Other development families include trained or partially fitted diagnostic targets, whose provenance is disclosed separately. The v0.1 suite remains sanity checks. Results do not establish improvements to a general-purpose language model, math capability or autonomous discovery of a looping mechanism.

The pilot contains eighteen sessions, six development problems per model, with one attempt each. Repeated models, episodes, parameterizations and observations do not increase the independent task count. Read and Write scores remain separate. The sample cannot support a headline ranking, precise generalization estimate or cross-vendor comparison. Non-OpenAI configurations have not completed a comparable measured pilot.

Exact success and partial agreement answer different questions. Every model failed the four exact Read tasks, while partial agreement differed substantially. Write's zero-start/no-edit control already attains 70.1% average accuracy because the outputs are imbalanced. Interpret target accuracy with affected-case accuracy, retention and exact success. A privileged reference proves representability and grading correctness; it does not prove a general solver will find the answer within budget.

Conventional baseline algorithms disclose their computation and information access. Some code-based decoders saturate B/C and therefore remain difficulty controls. They are not eligible condition-A model results and do not use an identical scaffold to the bounded model tool interface. Families with saturation or unresolved admission concerns remain diagnostic or candidate tasks.

The public examples and saved pilot submissions are exposed development material. Active held-out targets, answers and generation seeds remain private. API-exposed evaluation material is described as held out, without a guarantee against leakage. Development and held-out seeds can share a base; public exports exclude every original generation seed and seed-bearing private manifest. Source generators are public, while canonical held-out instances are not.

Condition A prohibits external tools, not internal reasoning. A trace cannot establish the absence of mental simulation or semantic forward emulation. Conditions B/C also require semantic trace review, beyond quotas or a sandbox. An approved audit means the recorded attempt passed protocol review; it does not mean its answer is correct. Public replay checks the released artifacts and scores, not unrecorded historical activity or model internals.

Model identifiers are provider aliases where no immutable snapshot is available. Record the upstream name, configuration and verification date. Alias changes can prevent exact future replication. The published latency is original local session elapsed time, including API round trips and harness work. CPU regrading does not reproduce that latency. Token-price estimates use the recorded usage and versioned prices, price cache categories separately, and include reasoning once within output. They are not provider invoices or total project costs.

Current reporting retains every prescribed attempt, including incorrect answers. Invalid submissions and protocol violations receive zero credit; infrastructure failures are reported separately. The measured eighteen-session cohort has none of those failures. Community runs remain separately labeled until their provenance, complete matrix, scores and traces pass review.

WSPBench is independent of the original weight-space-programming researchers, model providers and cited benchmarks. See [NOTICE](../NOTICE), [errata](../ERRATA.md) and the protocol documents for attribution and methodological history. Primary headline scoring and website work remain deferred.
